Supporting Underlying Metrics

To promote transparency and research, Treasury is releasing this large subset of aggregated data metrics (Supporting Underlying Metrics) supporting the analyses in the accompanying report.  Treasury believes the Supporting Underlying Metrics data set will provide useful information to stakeholders.

For more information concerning the collection and validation of the data generally, see Analyses of U.S. Homeowners Insurance Markets, 2018-2022: Climate-Related Risks and Other Factors (Report), available on FIO’s “Reports and Notices” webpage.  The Report includes information on definitions and calculations of metrics as well as additional context.  

The data from the Property and Casualty Market Intelligence Data Call  used in the Report (PCMI Data) does not include various components of the homeowners insurance market, all insurers, or all homeowners insurance policy forms.  It also does not include information on owner-occupied homeowners insurance policies that are provided by residual market plans or excess & surplus (E&S) insurance markets.  Flood insurance and reinsurance are also outside the scope of the PCMI Data.  In addition, the PCMI Data does not include data from 2023 and 2024.  

Also, while the PCMI Data for the Report is the broadest and most granular collection to date relating to homeowners insurance, its scope varies across states and ZIP Codes within states.  Generally, the collection targeted the largest insurers, but regulators in some states determined that additional smaller insurers domiciled in their states should also report.  Accordingly, the data is less representative in states that have more policies underwritten by smaller insurers that were not asked to report.  The PCMI Data includes 80 percent of HO-3 and HO-5 policies nationwide, as measured by direct premiums written, with market share by the same measure above 50 percent in 49 states, including almost complete coverage in a handful of states.  

The PCMI Data is based on a first-of-its-kind collaborative effort by the NAIC, state insurance regulators, and FIO.  Because this was a novel data-collection effort, the data collected required review and validation by the NAIC and state insurance regulators.  Some fields were collected for the first time during this data call, and the data call was also the first time most participating insurers had reported data nationwide at a ZIP Code level.  The data was also reported retrospectively for the five-year period from 2018 through 2022, which could lead to inconsistencies if insurers were not contemporaneously recording the data in a readily compatible form.  Prior to the Report’s publication, there remained certain data anomalies that may be subject to ongoing correction, which is not uncommon for a first-of-its-kind data collection.

Using larger groupings of data allows for greater statistical confidence than would be possible regarding granular areas, such as individual or small groups of ZIP Codes.  Given the broad, nationwide market coverage of 80 percent of premiums written for the most common homeowners insurance policies, the data can be informative for aggregate analyses of nationwide and regional developments, as in the Report.  The NAIC has also used the data collected through the PCMI Data Call to conduct overall market analyses.  See, e.g., NAIC, “State Insurance Regulators Monitor the Home Insurance Market to Protect Consumers,” news release, December 4, 2024, https://content.naic.org/article/state-insurance-regulators-monitor-home-insurance-market-protect-consumers (describing changes in the homeowners insurance market based on analyses of information from the PCMI Data Call).  On balance, due to the sample size of the data and given that the analyses in the Report use averages calculated over large numbers of aggregated ZIP Codes, the data was of sufficient quality to support the analyses conducted for the Report.  

Treasury’s review and validation of data was for the purposes of its aggregate analyses in the Report.  It did not validate the data in each ZIP Code presented.  The Supporting Underlying Metrics include certain data anomalies which may be of more limited materiality for purposes of aggregate analyses but which may be significant for results at the individual ZIP Code level.  

The Supporting Underlying Metrics are provided at the ZIP Code level, by year.  To protect the privacy of insurers and homeowners, only ZIP Codes with information from at least 10 reporting insurers and at least 50 insurance policies are included.  These limits reduce the total number of ZIP Codes by 22 percent from the PCMI Data underlying the Report.  As a result, the aggregate metrics in the Supporting Underlying Metrics will not completely align with the aggregate figures in the Report.  

The Supporting Underlying Metrics are those listed in the 'Metrics Definitions' tab.  They include the metrics analyzed in the Report and also Other Than Nonpayment Cancellation Rate.  

The workbook also includes an additional column for Policy Decile Group, which is a categorical variable providing a relative metric for the number of policies in a ZIP Code.  There is a wide range across ZIP Codes with respect to the number of policies represented.  To provide some information about the number of policies in each ZIP Code, ZIP Codes were grouped into deciles, 1 to 10, each of which corresponds to an equal number of ZIP Codes.  For example, Policy Decile Group 10 comprises the 10 percent of ZIP Codes with the largest numbers of policies and Policy Decile Group 1 comprises the 10 percent of ZIP Codes with the fewest policies (subject to a minimum of 50 policies).  Analysts may find this variable useful when aggregating data across the ZIP Codes or for comparing data across ZIP Codes.
